The Collapse of Capelin

For decades, capelin (Loðna) was one of the most important species in Icelandic fisheries and often made up the largest share of the total catch. The data shows that fluctuations in Iceland's total catch closely followed fluctuations in capelin landings, while other major species such as cod and haddock remained relatively stable. However, despite being caught in enormous quantities, capelin was rarely the most valuable species economically.

Beginning in the early 2000s, capelin catches started to decline significantly due to changing ocean conditions, weak recruitment, and stricter quota management. The decline strongly affected ports that depended heavily on pelagic fishing. In some years the stock became so weak that no capelin fishing was allowed at all. In Iceland, there was no capelin fishery in 2019, 2020, and 2024.

The capelin story highlights both the ecological volatility of Icelandic fisheries and the economic vulnerability of ports that relied heavily on a single species.
